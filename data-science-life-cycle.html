01

Decide what "done" means

Problem definition

Before any code, write down three things: the business question, the machine learning task it maps to, and the number that tells you the model is good enough. Skip this and you will build something accurate that nobody asked for.

Your turn

A property portal wants to show sellers an estimated price the moment they list a flat. Which framing is the right one?

The three lines we are committing to

Business goal — show a seller a fair asking price in under a second.
ML problem — supervised regression: predict a continuous price in lakhs from location, area, BHK, bath and balcony.
Success metric — mean absolute error under ₹15 lakh, and R² above 0.80 on held-out data.

# "2100 - 2850" and "34.46Sq. Meter" both become one float
def to_sqft(x):
    t = str(x).split("-")
    if len(t) == 2:
        return (float(t[0]) + float(t[1])) / 2
    try: return float(x)
    except: return None

df["bhk"] = df["size"].apply(lambda s: int(str(s).split(" ")[0]))
df = df[df.total_sqft / df.bhk >= 300]   # no 150 sqft bedrooms

07

Ask how wrong it is

Model evaluation

One score on one split is a rumour. Look at the error in rupees, not just R², check the residuals for a pattern, and repeat the whole fit across folds so you know the score was not luck.

Your turn

Read the metrics like this

MAE is the average miss in lakhs — the number you can say out loud to a seller.
RMSE punishes big misses harder, so if it is far above MAE you have a few bad blowouts.
R² is the share of price variation explained. Negative means you are worse than guessing the mean.

09

The part everyone forgets

Monitoring & maintenance

Your model froze the Bengaluru market on the day you trained it. The market did not freeze. Prices drift, new layouts appear, a metro line opens in Whitefield — and the error grows quietly while the API keeps returning confident numbers. Drag the months forward and watch it happen.

Your turn

Run the clock forward. Decide when you would retrain, then press the button and see if you were right.

The alert line is the promise you made in stage 1: mean absolute error under ₹15 lakh. Cross it and the model has broken its contract.

And then it starts again

Retraining sends you back to stage 2 for fresh data, stage 3 to clean it, stage 4 to check whether the relationships moved. The life cycle is a loop, not a checklist — which is why the last stage points at the first.
